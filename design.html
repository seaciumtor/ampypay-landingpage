02Typography

Inter, pushed hard at both ends. One family, self-hosted as a variable woff2. Headlines run heavy (800–900) with negative tracking and line-heights near 1.0; body copy stays quiet at 400–600. Hierarchy is built from reduced-opacity foreground color — never gray hex values.

Asymmetric grids, generous air, oversized screenshots. A 1200px container, two-column grids that are never quite 50/50, and product screenshots treated as the visual backbone — oversized, layered, clipped at the viewport edge, and lit from behind.

Screenshot treatment — four rules

OversizeHero shot is 130% width, bleeding off the right viewport edge; exceptions visual runs 114%. Impact over containment.
Layer2–3 overlapping shots with drop-shadows, staggered z-index and negative margins. On desktop the compliance collage layers move at −40/−90/−140px parallax.
GlowRadial rgba(37,99,234,…) gradients with blur(48px) sit behind every layered collage.
AnnotateGlassmorphic floating chips anchor real product claims to the imagery.

05Motion

GSAP + ScrollTrigger + Lenis + Three.js, all vendored locally. The rule: content is fully visible without JavaScript — animation only adds hidden states once libraries are confirmed present. Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion.

The vocabulary

Hero introTimeline: title words → eyebrow → sub → CTAs → badges → panel (8° rotateX) → chips.
Split headlineWords in masked spans · yPercent 110→0 · power4.out · stagger 0.045s.

Floating chipsInfinite ±12px sine bob, staggered phase per chip.

Hovers0.25–0.35s ease · lifts of −4 to −6px.
Hero particle waveThree.js · 130×70 blue additive points, layered sine displacement, camera eases toward mouse. Pauses off-screen.
Security globeThree.js · ~14k land-masked dots, pulsing data-center halos, orange great-circle arcs, drag-to-spin with inertia.

Easing — nothing linear except scrub

power4.outHeadline word reveals — arrives fast, lands soft
power3.outFade-up reveals, panel entrances
elastic.outMagnetic button release
cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1)Modal entrance (back / spring)

Under prefers-reduced-motion: Lenis off, all animation clamped to 0.01ms, preloader hidden, counters and rings render final values immediately, globe and wave hold still.

06Ornamentation & Three.js

Why the page carries WebGL scenes, glows, grain, and a custom cursor at all — and the rules that keep them from becoming noise. The guiding test: every decoration has to earn its place by meaning something. Payroll is global and security-critical, so the ornamentation argues exactly that — never decoration for its own sake.

The decision — why decorate a payroll site

The briefEnterprise payroll is perceived as dry and risky. The page has to feel like serious, modern infrastructure — credible enough to hand your payroll to — without tipping into playful fintech.
The betRestrained, purposeful motion signals engineering quality. A globe says "global, redundant infrastructure"; a particle field says "live, computational." The ornamentation is the argument, made visually.
The limitOne accent color, one typeface, two WebGL moments. Anything that doesn't carry meaning — or that costs the fundamentals (load, a11y, readability) — gets cut. Flourish is the exception, not the default.

Three.js scenes — two, and only two

SceneWhat it is & why it exists
Hero particle wave
#hero-canvas		130×70 grid of blue additive-blended points with layered sine displacement; the camera eases toward the mouse. Reads as something live and computational running behind the headline. Pauses via IntersectionObserver when off-screen.
Security globe
#globe-canvas		Dotted sphere (~14k points, Natural Earth land mask from js/world-land.js); pulsing amber data-center halos (Dallas, Paris, Bangkok, Singapore), orange great-circle arcs between regions, HTML provider-logo badges projected from 3D each frame, drag-to-spin with inertia + slow auto-rotation. Makes "global, redundant, secure" tangible in the Security section.

Guardrails — what keeps ornamentation honest

Earns its placeIf a decoration doesn't communicate something true about the product, it doesn't ship. Two scenes, not ten.
Never blocks contentPage is fully readable with zero JS. Three.js lazy-loads after the hero image to protect LCP; scenes only attach once hasThree is confirmed.
Costs nothing to readAll canvases, glows, grain, and the custom cursor are aria-hidden — decoration is invisible to assistive tech and carries no meaning that isn't also in text.
Respects the userprefers-reduced-motion stops globe auto-rotation and wave motion, clamps every transition to 0.01ms, and hides the preloader. Off-screen scenes pause to save battery and CPU.
Degrades to nothingNo WebGL, no GSAP, slow device — the content, layout, and color system stand on their own. Ornamentation is purely additive.
Stays vendoredGSAP, ScrollTrigger, Lenis, Three.js are local copies in js/vendor/ — no CDNs, no third-party requests for decoration.

Lighter ornamentation — the supporting cast

Radial glowsBlue rgba(37,99,234,…) blur(48px) gradients behind layered screenshots — implies depth and light without a literal light source.
Film grainA single tiled SVG noise overlay at ~4% opacity warms the flat navy so it doesn't read as digital-cold.
Glassmorphic chipsBackdrop-blur callouts that anchor real product claims onto the imagery — decoration that labels.
Custom cursorRing + dot pair, fine-pointer only. A small signal of craft; never required to operate anything.

Note for this reference page: none of the above is applied here. This document is deliberately plain — system fonts, no WebGL, no grain — so it documents the ornamentation without imitating it.

07Accessibility

Decoration is hidden; meaning is labeled. The flash never costs the fundamentals. Canvases, chips, and glows are aria-hidden; everything that carries meaning gets a name, a label, or a paired indicator.

Semantic landmarksheader, main, section, nav, footer; a single h1; anchor-linked section IDs.
Never color aloneStatus meaning always pairs a dot with a text label — blockers, warnings, anomalies.
Modal disciplinerole="dialog", aria-modal, labelled title, focus moves in, Escape and backdrop close, body scroll locks.
State stays in syncaria-expanded / aria-hidden track the burger menu; icon-only controls carry aria-label.
Visible focus2px blue outline with 2px offset on interactive elements.
Descriptive alt textEvery product screenshot describes what the UI actually shows — not "dashboard screenshot."

08Voice

Headlines are claims, not labels. Enterprise-confident, precise, never playful. Eyebrows name the capability area; headlines make the argument; footnotes summarize the benefit with "Built to…" / "Designed to…".

Claims

"Built for payroll — not adapted to it" — why
"Every payroll issue, visible before submission" — platform
"Know your payroll is ready — before you submit" — confidence
"We handle payroll. You handle growth." — tagline

Patterns

Do

  • Card copy: one bold noun-phrase title + one sentence.
  • Mark AI features consistently with the ✦ spark + "AI" tag.
  • Close sections with a "Designed to…" benefit footnote.

Don't

  • Write headlines that just name the section ("Features", "Security").
  • Make claims without the footer's forward-looking disclaimer in place.
  • Use playful fintech tone — this is serious infrastructure.

09Files

No build step. No modules. No CDNs. Plain HTML, CSS, and an IIFE. Libraries are vendored. Class names are kebab-case, describe components, and carry section prefixes.
